# 🧪 Lab 019 — Network Reconnaissance with Nmap: Port Discovery and Service Enumeration

**Day:** 025  
**Category:** Network Reconnaissance  
**Difficulty:** Beginner  
**Status:** Completed

---

# Objective

The objective of this lab was to understand how ethical hackers perform the reconnaissance phase of a security assessment using Nmap. Rather than interacting directly with services, the focus was on discovering hosts, identifying ports, recognizing running services, and understanding the information provided by different scan types.

---

# Environment

- Operating System: Ubuntu (WSL)
- Tool: Nmap 7.98
- Target: localhost (127.0.0.1)

---

# Tasks Performed

## Task 1 — Verify Installation

Verified that Nmap was installed correctly and identified the installed version.

---

## Task 2 — Basic Host Scan

Performed a standard scan against localhost to determine host availability and identify open TCP ports.

---

## Task 3 — Port Range Scan

Scanned ports 1–1024 to observe how Nmap reports the status of ports within a selected range.

---

## Task 4 — Service Version Detection

Performed service detection to understand how Nmap attempts to identify applications and software versions running on discovered services.

---

# Observations

- The localhost system responded successfully.
- No TCP services were actively listening on the scanned ports.
- All scanned ports were reported as closed.
- Service detection completed successfully despite the absence of open services.

---

# Knowledge Gained

Through this lab I learned:

- Difference between hosts and services.
- Difference between ports and applications.
- Importance of reconnaissance.
- Interpretation of closed ports.
- Basic understanding of service enumeration.
- How professional reconnaissance begins before exploitation.

---

# Challenges

Initially I expected every scan to reveal open services. After completing multiple scans I understood that scan results depend entirely on the services currently running on the target system.

---

# Conclusion

This lab strengthened my understanding of network reconnaissance and demonstrated that successful information gathering includes correctly interpreting both positive and negative scan results. Understanding a target environment before attempting interaction is an essential skill in ethical hacking.
