# 🎯 Objective

Today's objective was to strengthen my understanding of **network reconnaissance**, one of the most important phases of ethical hacking. Instead of directly interacting with network services, I focused on learning how ethical hackers first discover hosts, ports, and services using **Nmap**.

The emphasis was on understanding *why* reconnaissance comes before exploitation and how to correctly interpret scan results.

---

# 📚 Theory Learned

Today's learning introduced me to the fundamentals of network reconnaissance.

I learned that a computer may run multiple services simultaneously using a single IP address because each application communicates through its own **port number**. This reinforced the difference between an IP address, which identifies a machine, and a port, which identifies a specific service running on that machine.

I also learned that reconnaissance is the foundation of penetration testing. Rather than assuming where services exist, an ethical hacker systematically discovers open ports, identifies running services, and gathers information before attempting any interaction.

Another important concept was **service enumeration**. Simply knowing that a service exists is useful, but identifying its version provides much more valuable information for later security analysis.

# 🔍 Observations

The localhost scan showed that no TCP services were actively listening on the scanned ports.

Nmap correctly identified all scanned ports as closed rather than open. This demonstrated that successful reconnaissance does not always reveal open services, and that negative results are equally valuable because they accurately describe the target's current network exposure.

Service detection completed successfully even though no services were identified, reinforcing that Nmap attempts service enumeration regardless of whether open ports are found.

# ⭐ Biggest Learning

One of today's biggest realizations was that ethical hacking begins with investigation rather than exploitation.

Reconnaissance is about understanding a target before interacting with it. Even when no services are discovered, the collected information still contributes to building an accurate picture of the system.

I also learned that scan results containing only closed ports are still considered successful reconnaissance because they provide verified information rather than assumptions.

---

# ⚠ Challenges Faced

Initially, I expected every Nmap scan to reveal open services.

After comparing different scans, I understood that the results depend entirely on what the target system is actually running. A properly secured machine may expose no services at all, and that is completely normal.
